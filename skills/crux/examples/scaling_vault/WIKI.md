## Pages

### comparison

- [[data-vs-model-scaling\|Data scaling versus model scaling]] — Which lever pays more depends on which is currently binding — and the honest answer is usually 'the one you have been starving', which is why the comparison has to be made at a fixed budget.

### concept

- [[compute-optimal-training\|Compute-optimal training]] — For a fixed compute budget, model size and training-set size should be scaled in roughly equal proportion — a correction that showed a generation of large models had been trained on far too little data.
- [[data-quality\|Data quality and deduplication]] — Corpus size counts examples, not information — duplicates, near-duplicates and train-test overlap all inflate the apparent value of more data.
- [[data-repetition\|Data repetition and the data-constrained regime]] — What happens when more data is not available: repeated epochs are nearly as good as fresh data for a few passes, then decay to worthless.
- [[distribution-shift\|Distribution shift]] — An advantage measured in-distribution need not survive a change of source — and in-distribution accuracy turns out to predict robustness better than most interventions designed for it.
- [[irreducible-error\|Irreducible error]] — The additive constant in a scaling fit — the part of the loss that no amount of data or capacity removes — and why it decides whether scaling is worth funding.
- [[sample-efficiency\|Sample efficiency]] — How much accuracy a marginal example buys — the quantity a data-scaling claim is really about, and the one most often reported without a matched-budget control.
- [[scaling-laws\|Scaling laws]] — Generalisation error falls as a power law in training-set size, parameter count and compute, over many orders of magnitude and across domains — with a small exponent and an additive floor.
- [[transfer-learning\|Transfer learning]] — Pretrain once at scale, adapt many times — the setting in which a data-versus-model question is usually asked, and where pretraining scale dominates.

### dataset

- [[imagenet\|ImageNet]] — The 1.28M-image labelled benchmark that most scaling claims are still measured against, and its ~14M-image, ~21k-class superset.
- [[jft-300m\|JFT-300M and large weakly labelled corpora]] — The 300M-image internal corpus behind the 'performance grows logarithmically with data' result — and the qualification that is usually dropped when it is cited.
- [[text-corpora\|Text corpora — The Pile and C4]] — Two large public text corpora built on opposite philosophies — deliberate diversity versus filtered scale — and what that difference does to a scaling measurement.
- [[web-scale-image-text\|Web-scale image-text corpora]] — Hashtag- and caption-supervised corpora at billions of examples — the largest public evidence on data scaling, and the clearest case of returns depending on what the data is *of*.

### entity

- [[efficientnet\|EfficientNet and compound scaling]] — Depth, width and resolution scale better together than separately — the model-side analogue of the compute-optimal result.
- [[resnet\|ResNet]] — The residual convolutional family used here as the smaller, well-understood arm — the model whose behaviour at modest data scale is best characterised.
- [[transformer-language-models\|Transformer language models]] — The setting where scaling laws were established most cleanly — and where inference cost has since pushed practice deliberately away from compute-optimal training.
- [[vision-transformer\|Vision Transformer]] — The higher-capacity arm: weaker than convolutions at small data, stronger at large — the clearest case of a model comparison whose answer depends entirely on data scale.

### method

- [[compute-budget\|Compute budget]] — Training compute as the quantity that must be held fixed for a data-versus-model comparison to mean anything, and how it is counted.
- [[controlled-comparison\|Controlled comparison]] — The discipline of changing one thing: what has to be held fixed for a data-versus-model result to license the conclusion it states.
- [[data-pruning\|Data pruning]] — Removing the least informative examples can improve the scaling exponent itself — evidence that the power law is a property of random sampling, not a hard limit.
- [[fine-tuning\|Fine-tuning]] — The adaptation protocol held fixed across every arm — end-to-end updates on the downstream task, with the schedule specified in advance.
- [[held-out-evaluation\|Held-out evaluation]] — Test-set reuse, and the evidence that headline accuracy drops sharply on a freshly collected test set drawn the same way.
- [[seeds-and-variance\|Seeds and run-to-run variance]] — How large a difference has to be before it means anything, and why a single-seed comparison at these effect sizes is uninterpretable.
